Measurement details

Pose accuracy

Each task contains 100 image pairs. Accuracy is evaluated on the RTX 5070 Ti for all supported matcher, resolution and precision configurations. We follow the SuperGlue pose evaluation code: the pose error of a pair is the larger of its rotation error and translation-direction error. Translation is evaluated as an angular error, with the sign ambiguity of the essential matrix accounted for.

Pose AUC integrates the cumulative fraction of all pairs whose error is below the threshold, normalized over 0–5°, 0–10° or 0–20°. Failed estimates remain in the denominator with infinite error. Success ratio reports the fraction of pairs with both angular errors at or below the selected threshold; it is distinct from AUC.

Calibrated image coordinates are undistorted and transformed with the evaluated image resolution. The reference estimator uses OpenCV RANSAC, a 1-pixel threshold in the resized image, confidence 0.99999 and at least five returned correspondences. Reference pose recovery and AUC integration are used for every matcher.

Inputs and matcher configurations

Image resolutions are 128, 256, 512 and 1024 pixels on the longest side. The same task inputs and pose evaluation are used across matchers. FP32 uses full precision; MP uses automatic mixed precision; FP16 uses half-precision inference in supported pipelines. ORB and SIFT use their native numerical formats. DKM and MASt3R do not support the evaluated FP16 mode.

MatchAnything denotes the ELoFTR-based variant. Visible STheReO images use the author's BGGR Bayer conversion. RUBIK images retain their original RGB representation, and thermal images retain the benchmark's fixed preprocessing. The input manifest provides all 400 pair identifiers, image paths relative to their datasets, and image hashes.

Common image resizing uses Pillow BILINEAR. Matcher-specific processing follows the fixed settings below. No additional common cap is imposed on returned correspondences. TF32 is disabled for learned pipelines.

Runtime

Each configuration is prepared with 3 seconds of idle time, 10 initial warm-up calls and two passes over the 16 resource pairs (four per task), for 42 warm-up calls. Runtime is measured with CUPTI disabled. We take the median of three calls for each pair and then the median across the four pairs in the task.

The measured interval includes matcher-specific preprocessing, CPU–GPU transfers, matching, returned correspondences, result checks and completion synchronization. Common image loading and resizing, model loading, warm-up and pose estimation lie outside it.

GPU memory

CUPTI records CUDA allocation and release events from before model loading. The reported value is the median of the four per-pair peak process allocations during the measurement interval after warm-up. It includes allocations still live at that time, such as model storage. MiB are divided by 1024 for display in GiB. Clean runtime uses a separate container from the memory and energy measurements.

Energy per pair

The same pair is matched repeatedly for at least 6 seconds and at least 5 calls. Power is sampled at 100 ms intervals, integrated over the active interval, and divided by the actual number of calls. Idle power is not subtracted. The reported energy is the median across the four pairs. Energy runs with CUPTI disabled after memory measurement in the resource process.

OOM and unavailable modes

All 1,344 resource rows are downloadable. The 56 Orin OOM rows have missing measurements and zero valid pairs, rather than zero cost. Unsupported precision combinations are distinct from measured configurations that ran out of memory. Configuration filtering retains every option meeting the selected limits and highlights the highest positive pose AUC.
